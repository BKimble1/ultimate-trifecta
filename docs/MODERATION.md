# Names, chat and moderation (V6)

This document describes what the game and its service actually do about
player names and communication, where reports go, and what happens when the
service isn't there. Filtering can't make a game safe on its own. These
filters work together with Mute, Block, reports to a person, and the
owner's moderation actions.

**Status, October 2026.** Everything below is implemented and tested
locally: Godot tests, plus the service's `npm test` against an in-memory
D1. **The service is not deployed.** The shipped build has no service URL,
so it runs in the service-off mode described in
[When the service is off](#when-the-service-is-off). Typed chat, the report
queue and server-side name approval only become live once the owner deploys
`service/` and fills in `game/config/service.cfg` (see
[Owner setup](#owner-setup)).

## Names

### Policy (one policy, two places)

The service (`service/src/names.js`) is the only authority that **approves**
a name for online play. The game runs the same policy on the device
(`game/src/core/name_rules.gd`, using the same word lists from
`ModerationTerms`, which is generated by `service/tools/build_terms.py`).
The device copy has three jobs: instant feedback while you type, checking
names saved by older versions, and checking every name that arrives from
another device. A shared corpus fixture checks that both copies decide the
same way. It currently has 476 names and 622 messages, made up of ordinary
names, harmless look-alikes and evasions
(`game/tests/data/moderation_fixture.json`, regenerated by
`service/tools/export_policy_fixture.mjs` and checked by
`test_moderation`).

Steps, in order:

1. **Normalise.**
   - NFKC on the service. On the device, full-width letters are folded,
     which is the part of NFKC that matters for names.
   - Invisible format characters are removed: zero-width spaces and joiners,
     direction marks and overrides, the soft hyphen and the word joiner.
   - Whitespace runs become one space, and the name is trimmed.
2. **Shape.**
   - 3–16 characters: letters, digits, single spaces or underscores.
   - At least two letters, and no runs of 6 or more digits.
   - **Unicode policy:** names are ASCII letters and digits only. Look-alike
     letters from other scripts (Cyrillic "а", Greek "ο") can spell anything
     past a word list, so the name rules refuse them. Chat allows accented
     Latin letters (see below).
3. **Whole words.**
   - The name is split on spaces, underscores, digit runs and lower→Upper
     changes ("BigBoss" → "big boss"), and is also read with leet digits as
     letters ("m0d", "B0t").
   - A word made only of digits stays a number: "Otter 99" isn't "gg".
4. **Skeleton substrings.**
   - The skeleton is lower case, with leet digits read as letters (two
     readings for 1/0/6), separators removed, "rn" read as "m" and "vv" as
     "w", and repeated letters collapsed.
   - Harmless words spelled out in full are cut out first (the ALLOW list:
     Scunthorpe, cocktail, therapist, raccoon, Sussex, Nigel, Japan, …).
5. **Categories.**
   - Slurs, sexual content, profanity, threats, impersonation of staff, the
     game or a bot ("Admin", "Trifecta Staff", "B0t"), and contact details or
     links ("www", "discord", "insta", "add me").
6. **Reserved names.** "Player", "Guest", "Night Watch", "Blocked player"
   and the owner's reserved list (an admin action; names are reserved
   automatically after a forced rename).

**V6 fix: false positives.** V5's rule matched collapsed forms of terms with
doubled letters, so a term could collapse into a common fragment ("…oo…" →
"…o…"). That wrongly rejected **Bob, Bacon, Iconic, Second and Contact**. It
also read "Otter 99" and "Frog 10" as contact handles. Now a collapsed term
is matched only while it stays distinctive (4+ letters, or a term without
doubled letters). Other terms are matched as spelled, with stretched letters
squeezed to two ("cooooon" still matches). All of these now pass, and each
has a test (`names.test.mjs`, `test_moderation`).

**Refusals.** A refused name gets a friendly reason that never repeats the
name ("Names can't look like staff, the game or a bot.") and three safe
suggestions that are one tap away (Name sheet). The service also gives a
`#1234` discriminator to duplicate names, applies a 24 h rename cooldown,
and allows 6 name attempts per 10 minutes.

### Where names are shown

- **Received names.** Every name that arrives from another device is shown
  only if it passes the policy (`NameRules.safe_display`). Otherwise a stable
  curated stand-in for that player is shown. This covers the party roster,
  round start, results, series standings and chat. Names are always drawn as
  plain text (Labels, never markup).
- **Saved names** from older versions are checked against the whole policy
  when the profile loads (`Save.fit_name`). A failing name is replaced with
  a generated one.
- **Game-authored names** are fixed, reviewed lists:
  - curated player names ("Sleepy Otter 42"; every combination is tested,
    and a pair that spells a term across the words, such as "Sneaky Seal",
    is excluded);
  - bot names ("Snooze", "Pillow", …), always marked BOT;
  - item, dorm, pass and menu names are written by the team.
- **Blocked players** are shown as "Blocked player" everywhere this device
  draws a name: roster, stage nameplates, round reveal, map, feed, chat and
  results.

### Without the service: curated names for shared play

Without the moderation service, no person reviews names and nobody can
report them. So a typed custom name is never broadcast in a party.

- **Your party name.** In parties you appear under your saved name if it is
  one of the game's curated names, or else under a stable curated name made
  from your device ID (`Save.party_name`). The name sheet says so.
- **What the host enforces.** In such a party the host shows every member
  under a curated name: the name they sent if it is curated, otherwise a
  stand-in.
- **What every device enforces.** Every device applies the same rule to
  names it receives (`NameRules.party_display`), so a modified client or
  host can't push a custom name onto anyone's screen.

## Chat

### Quick Chat (always on in parties)

Quick Chat phrases are written by the team. They travel as numeric IDs, so
nobody can change what they say, and each phrase is offered only on the
channels where it makes sense (`game/src/core/quick_chat.gd`):

| Channel | Who hears it | When | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Party | everyone in the party | the party room, and the loading screen | Ready! · One moment · Let's play! · Be right back · Change the settings? |
| Team | your own role only (runners or Night Watch) | during a round | Need help · Heading home · Night Watch nearby! (runners) · Runner spotted! · Taking a cart (Night Watch) |
| Spectators | finished runners and people watching | during a round, once you're home | Go, go, go! · So close! · Wow! |
| Everyone | everyone | results after a round | Good game! · Well played · Rematch? · Thanks for playing! |

**Spectators can't relay what they see.** A runner who got home (or a
watcher) sees the whole campus. If they could still use team chat, they
could tell active teammates where the Night Watch is. So they can only talk
to other spectators. A captured runner keeps their normal, filtered view and
keeps team chat. There is no global or anonymous chat and no voice.

### Typed chat (only with a working moderation service)

Typed chat is offered only when all of these are true:

- the service is configured and advertises the `chat` feature;
- you are signed in;
- the party was set up through the service (a private party with a code or
  an invite, where every member was admitted by the service).

When any of these is missing, the drawer says why ("Typed chat needs the
game's online moderation service, which isn't set up in this build. Quick
Chat works.") and Quick Chat carries on.

How a typed message travels:

1. **On the device.** `ChatRules` checks the message for instant feedback.
   A refused message shows its reason under the field, and the text is
   never posted.
2. **On the service.** `POST /v1/chat/check` runs the same policy
   (`service/src/chat_rules.js`). It checks that the sender is a member of
   that live room (not left, not removed) and applies rate limits: 8 tries
   per 10 s and 150 per hour per profile, with refusals counted too. It then
   signs the approved text into a 5-minute RS256 token, using the admission
   key the game already ships. The service doesn't store the message.
3. **The host relays** the message only if the token verifies for this room
   and this sender's own profile, matches the channel it is sent on, and
   hasn't been used before.
4. **Every receiver verifies the token again** and re-checks the text
   against the policy before drawing it. A modified host can't put
   unapproved text on anyone's screen.

**The message policy (`ChatRules` / `chat_rules.js`):**

- up to 100 characters;
- printable ASCII, Latin-1 and Latin Extended-A letters, curly quotes and
  the ellipsis only (no emoji, no other scripts);
- no control characters;
- no markup (`< > [ ] { } \` and the backtick);
- no spam (a character 7 or more times, or a word 5 or more times in a row);
- no links, e-mail addresses, @handles, phone numbers (7 or more digits),
  contact apps or "add me"-style phrases;
- the abuse lists, checked on:
  - every word;
  - every word with its separators removed ("sh.it", "f*ck");
  - runs of single letters ("f u c k");
  - threat phrases across 2–3 words ("kill you").

  Leetspeak (including `@ $ !` inside words), stretched letters and
  harmless words are handled as for names. "gg" is fine: real links are
  caught by the link patterns, not by the word "gg".

### The host as referee (every message)

Implemented in `game/src/net/chat_channel.gd`. These rules apply to Quick
Chat and typed messages alike:

- **Who may send.** Only a seated human may send. Bots and room spectators
  don't chat.
- **Which channel.** The channel must be the one the sender may use right
  now, decided from the host's phase and the sender's state in the
  simulation. The phrase must fit the channel and the sender's role.
- **Rate limits.** A burst of 4, then one message every 2 s. The same phrase
  twice within 3 s is dropped. On top of that, the session's flood limit
  removes a peer that floods.
- **Refusals** go back to the sender only.

### On every receiver (defence)

- Chat is accepted only from the bound host.
- Messages are de-duplicated and ordered by the host's sequence number.
- The sender must be a seated human in the current roster.
- The channel must be one this device may see.
- A phrase must fit its channel and role, and a typed message's token is
  verified again.
- At most 8 messages per sender per 10 s are kept.
- Muted and blocked senders are dropped before anything is stored.
- History keeps the last 80 messages. A rejoining player receives the
  recent party conversation they're allowed to see.

### Where chat appears

- **Party room.** A Chat button with an unread count, bubbles over the
  sender's head, and the drawer.
- **Round.** A chat button beside Pause; it is a reserved touch region, so
  it never starts the stick or the camera. A three-line feed sits at the top
  right under the minimap, clear of the screen centre and the thumb
  controls, and fades after 6 s. The drawer can be opened from the button.
- **Results.** Chat in the actions row.

**The drawer and input.** Opening the drawer takes input ownership
(`InputOwner`). The round reads no movement, tag or jump while it is open,
and walking in the party room stops. The touch controls are released, and
when the drawer closes the press and touch state is cleared, so nothing
"sticks". The text field sits at the top of the drawer so the iOS keyboard
never covers it.

## Mute, block and report

| Action | Effect | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Mute** (this party) | Hides that player's chat and emotes, including what is already on screen | player card, chat drawer |
| **Block** | On this device, at once: hides their chat, emotes and name ("Blocked player") everywhere. On the service when signed in: you aren't put in parties together and they can't join yours. A host's block also removes them from the party | player card, chat drawer (with a confirmation) |
| **Report player** | Name, harassment, cheating, inappropriate behaviour, something else | player card, chat drawer |
| **Report message** | A typed message: harassment, inappropriate, spam, something else | chat drawer |

The block list is kept on the device (Settings › Profile lists it, with
Unblock) and on the service. Deleting the profile removes it.

### Reports: honest states and a real destination

`ReportSheet` shows the report's progress honestly:

1. **Choose a reason.**
2. **Sending…** The sheet can't be dismissed while the request is out.
3. **Sent.** Shown only once the service has confirmed the report. It shows
   the receipt (`R-XXXXXXXXXX`).

If the request fails, the sheet says why and offers **Try again**. Nothing is
ever shown as "reported" unless the service has it.

**Destination.** Reports go to the owner's moderation queue in the service
database:

- `POST /v1/reports` files a player or name report.
- `POST /v1/reports/message` files a message report. The service-signed
  message token is the evidence. The service checks it by signing it again,
  so a forged or edited message can't be reported against someone.
- A duplicate report returns the same receipt. Each reporter may file 12
  reports a day. Messages can be reported for 24 h after they were sent.

**What is stored for a report:**

- the reporter's and target's profile IDs, and the target's name at the
  time;
- the reason and optional details (up to 500 characters);
- context: room code, build, and for message reports the channel and send
  time;
- for a message report, the reported message itself (up to 200 characters)
  and its token ID.

### The owner's workflow

`service/tools/admin.mjs` gives the owner these commands:

```sh
node tools/admin.mjs queue            # open reports, oldest first; message reports print the message
node tools/admin.mjs show <profile>   # profile, recent names, reports against them
node tools/admin.mjs dismiss <receipt> [note]
node tools/admin.mjs rename <receipt> [note]       # forced rename; the old name becomes reserved
node tools/admin.mjs suspend <receipt> <hours> [note]   # no online play until it ends (rooms close)
node tools/admin.mjs unsuspend <profile>
node tools/admin.mjs reserve <name>
node tools/admin.mjs audit 50
```

Every action is written to the audit log. A reporter can check a receipt's
status (`GET /v1/reports/:id`: received or reviewed).

**Owner decision needed.** Before the App Store, the owner sets a review
cadence (for example, the queue checked daily) and a contact address. The
game shows `SUPPORT_EMAIL` / `SUPPORT_URL` from the service config when they
are set, and shows no placeholder otherwise.

### Profile deletion

Settings › Profile › Delete Game Profile removes these from the service:

- the profile and its identity link;
- name history and blocks;
- the quoted text of reported messages that the profile sent.

The player's own reports stay for moderators, without the link to them. Open
reports against the player are closed as "profile deleted". The device's
data is erased.

## When the service is off

This is the shipped state until the owner deploys the service:

- **Quick Chat** works in every party.
- **Typed chat** is unavailable, and the drawer says why. Nothing is sent
  anywhere.
- **Names** in parties are curated (see above). A name can still be chosen
  and is checked on the device, but parties show the curated name.
- **Reports.** The report sheet says reports aren't available and that
  nothing has been sent, and points to Mute and Block. It never shows
  "Reported".
- **Mute and block** work on the device. A host's block removes the player
  from the host's party.

## Limits (what this does not do)

- **Word lists.** They are English-centred, and no list catches every
  spelling. Leetspeak, separators, stretched letters, invisible characters
  and full-width letters are handled. Deliberately creative misspellings,
  other languages, and abuse that uses ordinary words are left to reports.
- **The host relays chat.** The host could withhold messages or drop
  players, but it can't forge typed text (receivers verify the service's
  signature) or invent phrases (they are IDs). Quick Chat flooding by a
  malicious host is capped on each receiver.
- **Text is checked, not watched.** Typed chat is checked before it is
  delivered. The service keeps no transcript: moderators see a message only
  when someone reports it.
- **No voice chat, no global chat**, no messages to strangers. Parties are
  private (a code or an invite).
- **Approval tokens** live 5 minutes. A message the host relays later is
  refused.

## Owner setup

1. **Deploy the service** (`service/README.md`). Its migrations include
   `0002_social_moderation.sql`.
2. **Configure contact details.** Set `SUPPORT_EMAIL` and `SUPPORT_URL` to
   real, monitored contacts.
3. **Configure the build.** Put the service URL and the admission public key
   in `game/config/service.cfg`, then rebuild.
4. **Staff the queue.** Check it regularly with `tools/admin.mjs queue`.
5. **App Store answers.** Update the privacy answers to the "with service"
   column in `docs/APP_STORE.md`.

## Tests

- **Service** (`cd service && npm test`):
  - `names.test.mjs`: the categories, evasions and false positives
    (including the V6 fixes).
  - `chat.test.mjs`: the message policy; approval only for members of a
    live room; rate limits; message reports with signed evidence;
    duplicates; tampering; self-reports; stale messages; deletion.
- **Game** (`tools/run_tests.sh <name>`):
  - `test_moderation`: parity with the service on the shared corpus,
    harmless names, suggestions, safe display, curated names, saved-name
    revalidation, and the name sheet.
  - `test_chat`: delivery, order and duplicates; the host's channel, phrase,
    rate and repeat limits; forged packets; signed, tampered, replayed and
    other people's messages; a bad host's unsigned or abusive relays; mute
    and block; reconnect history; and round channels (team only, spectators
    only, everyone after results).
  - `test_match_chat`: the feed shows your team only, and the drawer owns
    input (no move, tag or jump; nothing sticks).
  - `test_report_block`: honest report states with retry, the service-off
    sheet, and blocks.
  - `test_trust`: received names are sanitised.
