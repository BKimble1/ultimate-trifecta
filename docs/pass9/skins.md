# Pass 9 skins: Record Breaker and Dr. Doom

Workstream: brief §2–5 and the skins' part of §8 (complete character skins,
their faces, fit and motion). Branch `p9-skins`, base `2fff573`. Media:
[`docs/media/pass9/skins/`](../media/pass9/skins/).

**What these notes can claim.** Geometry, weights and containment were
measured on the generator's output with Blender's bpy on the real rig and
clips; the game-side behaviour was tested headless at a fixed 60 Hz through
the real `CharacterView`; pictures and reels are Godot 4.7.2's Mobile
renderer on Mesa llvmpipe under Xvfb. That shows shape, colour, fit, timing
and memory allocation sizes. It says nothing about frame rate, heat, touch
feel or a phone's resident memory: no device was used.

**Privacy.** Both skins are likenesses of real people from photos the owner
supplied. No name, title, institution, place or text from those photos is in
the repository (code, comments, docs, commit messages, file names, image
labels); the skins are only "Record Breaker" and "Dr. Doom". No photo or crop
is in the repository. Every comparison picture in git puts the owner's
**concept art** (an art reference) beside the engine render; checks against
the photos were made outside the repository and are not kept.

### Likeness translation (no identities)

The photos set the likeness; the concept art set the outfits; the stylised
concepts and the existing runners set the rounded presentation. Each head is
the game's rounded head shell reshaped by a smooth displacement field
(`HeadShape`: a few broad gaussian forms, faded out at the neck so every
head meets the shared neck seam), then given its own eyes, lids, brows,
nose, mouth, cheeks and ears. Nothing is a photo or a texture.

- **Record Breaker:** the smile is the identity: a wide D-shaped mouth (7 cm
  wide at rest on the game's head scale, wider in the smile key) with lifted
  corners, an upper row of teeth, fuller lifted cheeks and smile lines; the
  lower face is narrowed slightly (a soft jaw) and the chin lengthened and
  rounded; the brows are thick, low and nearly straight with a small arch;
  the eyes sit a little closer together and higher than the stock head's
  (to keep the photos' brow–nose–mouth spacing), with relaxed upper lids.
  The hair follows the photos first: volume on top, swept up and back off
  the forehead, a loose lock or two in front, fuller sides than the nape.
- **Dr. Doom:** a broad face that widens toward the lower cheeks, a higher
  rounded crown and broad forehead, heavier upper lids with a slight

  nose, ears scaled up 26 % and turned out, a closed smile whose corners
  lift into creases, and a soft chin form. The bald crown and the low
  fringe band carry the silhouette from behind and in profile.
- Both faces stay recognisably different from each other and from the stock
  head at gameplay distance (`lineup_*.jpg`, bottom rows).

## The complete-skin override

`Cosmetics` (small, explicit, additive):

- `COMPLETE_SKINS` lists each skin's head part, its own skin tone and hair
  tone, its resting face (none: the likeness is in the head's construction)
  and the copy that says what it replaces. `is_complete_skin`, `head_part`,
  `override_note`, `look_skin_color`, `look_hair_color` and
  `look_face_keys` are the only new functions.
- `runner_parts()` returns just the skin's three parts while one is worn:
  no `base` head and body, no hair, hat, shoes or marks. So there is never a
  second head, stock hair or duplicate footwear under it, and unsupported
  hats cannot hide or clip the silhouette.
- **Nothing in the saved appearance changes.** Hair, hair colour, hat,
  shoes, face, brows, marks, skin tone, colours and pattern stay saved and
  come back exactly with any other outfit; wearing a skin grants nothing
  else (no hat, hair or shoe item). Tested over every hat × shoes × hair ×
  marks combination, through the real `CharacterView` swap and restore, and
  through `Save.apply_appearance` with a device-ownership fixture.
- **Tint rule.** A complete skin's skin vertices take the skin's own tone
  (Record Breaker `(0.98, 0.80, 0.68)`, Dr. Doom `(0.96, 0.76, 0.64)`,
  chosen against the references in the dorm, campus and studio light); every
  other colour (hair, brows, eyes, cloth, leather, metal) is authored in the
  vertex colours with no tint selector, so the player's skin tone, hair
  colour, outfit colours and pattern cannot change the likeness. The
  player's face preset and brows do not apply either; marks are not drawn.
- **Night Watch:** unchanged rule. A Night Watch always wears the uniform
  with the player's own modular head, hair colour, skin tone and face, so a
  skin owner on the Watch is never a suited or shirtless cop
  (`CharacterView` passes the player's tones when `patrol`).
- **Bots** never wear a complete skin (`bot_cosmetic` maps one to Pajamas):
  they are Season Premium rewards.
- **No gameplay effect.** Appearance is view-only: the same capsule, speed,
  jump, reach, collision, camera and detection. A scripted simulation
  (240 ticks, three players moving and jumping) gives identical position
  traces with the two skins and with the default look (`test_skins_p9`).
  Nothing in the skins touches the simulation, collision or camera code.
- **Copy.** The Locker shows a note at the top of the outfit tab (the
  whole rule) and of the colours, face, hair, hat and shoes tabs (that this
  pick is covered while the skin is worn and saved for other outfits). The
  Shop's includes text for these items appends the includes line and the
  override sentence. The Season stream's previews can use
  `Cosmetics.override_note()`.

`CharacterView` (small): a `face_mesh` and per-part blend-shape index map;
`_apply_cosmetics` binds expressions to the shown head (`_bind_face`) and the
new head starts from the current expression, so a swap never shows a frame
of a neutral face; `show_face()` sets a still face for portraits and
captures. `Portraits` uses `show_face`. Lobby, gameplay, spectator, results,
Locker and Shop previews all draw through `CharacterView`, so the skin and
its face appear in all of them.

## Faces and expressions

Each head carries the stock face keys, in the stock order (`blink`,
`squint`, `smile`, `open`, `brow_up`, `brow_angry`, `face_bright`,
`face_sleepy`, `brow_flat`), built for that face: every moving vertex is a
function of a few face parameters (lid opening and tilt, brow raise, inner
and outer brow, mouth width, corner lift, mouth opening, cheek raise), and
each key is the head rebuilt with its parameters moved. Lids are real skin
shells that close over the eye along the lid edge, lashes ride the lid
edge, and the mouth interior and teeth fold away when closed. The shapes
were exported as glTF morph targets and checked on the imported mesh
(`expressions.jpg`; every key moves its features in `test_skins_p9`).

The existing expression system now plays on the active head, so:

| Moment | Record Breaker | Dr. Doom |
|---|---|---|
| Idle | his broad toothy smile at rest, an extra small smile | the closed, knowing smile; a small amused extra smile |
| Blink | full blink every 2.2–4.6 s (slower, sometimes double in menus) | full blink under the heavier lids |
| Moving at full speed / tag | brows down, smile off (focused) | brows down, smile off (focused) |
| Tagged (captured) | surprise: mouth open, brows up | surprise: lips part, brows up |
| Results, celebrate, cheer | squint + broad smile + open mouth | squint + smile + parted lips (pleased) |
| Emotes | the emote's own face (wave, shush, stargaze, moon shuffle) | same |

Face weights are lerped (no pops) and the identity does not drift: no key
changes the head's shape outside the eyes, brows, mouth and cheeks. No lobby
flourish (lapel adjustment, cuff check) was added: that needs new clips in
the animation stream's files and was out of scope.

## Construction, materials, skinning

- **One shared material.** All six parts use the shared character
  `ShaderMaterial` (one-sided). Material classes: skin (also Dr. Doom's
  fringe), cloth, V8 satin (the jacket lining and the tie),
  rubber/leather (sandals, belt, soles, wristband), V8 metal (buckle),
  gloss (eyes, buttons, shoes), lit gloss (teeth, eye whites and Record
  Breaker's shorts). One surface per part.
- **Weights.** Garments use the generator's established helpers (`torso_w`,
  `arm_w`, `leg_w`, the head rigid). Three decisions made the layers hold:
  1. **Bare legs and trouser legs start inside the garment above them**
     (Record Breaker's legs at 0.45 m inside the shorts' legs; Dr. Doom's
     trouser legs at 0.50 m inside the seat), so a thigh's skin and its
     cover share one weight field where they overlap.
  2. **One hips field for Dr. Doom's lower layers** (`lower_w`): the
     trousers' seat, the tops of the trouser legs and the jacket skirt all
     blend from the torso's weights to the thighs' by the same function of
     position, more toward the thigh at the front. Layers that move by the
     same weights cannot cross.
  3. **A softer hips/spine blend for the suit torso** (`dd_tw`), so the
     jacket's waist does not pinch when the chest turns.
- **Attachments share the limb's weights.** The wristband is weighted by
  the arm's own weight function at its position (`arm_w`), and the sandals
  by the same foot weight function as the bare foot under them (`foot_w`).
- **The tie** is weighted to the chest and lies 2–5 mm off the shirt; it has
  no secondary motion, so it is bounded by construction (measured below).
- **Normals.** The build's sharp-edge rule plus analytic normals on the
  shirt stripes, eye and lid shells.

## White shorts: cause and measurements

**Symptom.** The shorts read pale blue in studio light and light grey in
the dorm. **Cause, measured.** Their albedo was already white (`#f5f4ef`,
0.91 linear), so neither albedo nor roughness was the issue. They were in
the cloth class, which has low specular and no self-light, and the
character shader also adds a blue rim sheen to cloth outdoors. The cloth
therefore shows only what the scene lights it with. Every scene's fill is
cool (the studio's ambient `#8fa3c8` and blue fill light, campus
moonlight), and the studio and dorm use filmic tonemapping, which keeps a
lit white well below display white. The Moonwalk Cadet's white suit (also
cloth) measures the same as the old shorts in the same render (table). The
game's whites that do read white are the eye whites and teeth: the "lit
gloss" class, which adds a little neutral self-light (0.35 × albedo).
**Change.** The shorts, waistband and seams use that class at very high
roughness (0.92–0.94, so its specular is a broad soft sheen, not a gloss),
with the same near-white albedo; only `skins_p9.py` changed. They now match
the eye whites in the same render and do not glow: they stay below the
eye white in every light and keep their seams, ribs and fold shading.

Mean sRGB of the shorts' lit front panel (the right-hand box in
[`shorts_white_before_after.jpg`](../media/pass9/skins/shorts_white_before_after.jpg);
data and boxes in [`data/shorts_white.json`](../media/pass9/skins/data/shorts_white.json)).
Same camera and lights before and after; desktop renders:

| Light | Shorts before | Shorts after | Eye white (same render) | Cadet white suit (cloth) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dorm | 99, 93, 107 (grey) | 174, 171, 174 | 172, 171, 176 | 93, 87, 100 |
| Campus night | 156, 173, 226 (blue) | 224, 230, 252 | 230, 236, 254 | 162, 175, 221 |
| Studio | 110, 125, 148 (blue-grey) | 177, 182, 189 | 179, 183, 191 | 108, 121, 140 |

The remaining blue in campus night is the moonlight's own colour, and the
eye whites carry the same tint. In each light the shorts are now the
brightest, most neutral surface on the body after the eyes.

**Dr. Doom's fringe** had the same cause. It was a mid grey-brown in the
cloth class and read charcoal: a back-of-head patch measured RGB 66, 55, 61
in the dorm. It now uses the skin's material class (warm wrap light and a
little transmission, with no skin tint) and a lighter warm grey-brown
(`#c6ad93` body, `#a48a72` shadow, `#e4d3bd` light). It reads warm grey,
not charcoal, in all three lights (`lineup_*.jpg`).

## Containment and fit through every clip

`tools/character/skins_check.py` (new) deforms the skins exactly as the game
does (linear blend skinning with the four strongest, renormalised weights
of the exported mesh) by every clip (53) sampled at 20 Hz, and measures real
deformed vertices against real deformed faces. `outfit_check.py` runs it
too (a small additive hook). Data:
[`data/skins_check.json`](../media/pass9/skins/data/skins_check.json).
Result: **0 failures**; `outfit_check.py` and `clip_check.py`: 0 failures.

| Check | Limit | Worst over every clip |
|---|---|---|
| Record Breaker: bare thighs and hips inside the shorts (seat and legs) | 1.0 cm | 0.0 cm |
| Dr. Doom: shirt inside the jacket where the jacket covers it | 1.0 cm | 0.7 cm (hard landing) |
| Dr. Doom: trousers inside the jacket skirt | 1.0 cm | 0.99 cm (hard landing, one sample); tag lunge 0.38, tag miss 0.49 |
| Dr. Doom: hip crease, jacket over trousers (exception below) | 4.5 cm | 4.05 cm (sprint) |
| Dr. Doom: tie blade off the shirt (no sinking, no lifting away) | 0–1.6 cm | 0.23–0.50 cm |
| Record Breaker: wristband stays on the wrist (drift) | 0.2 cm | 0.055 cm |
| Record Breaker: sandals and every foot vertex together | 0.2 cm | 0.0 cm |
| Arms against each skin's head and hair, vs the stock head with curls | baseline + 1.0 cm | within; worst excess: shush (below) |

Method: each inner-layer vertex is anchored at rest to the cover straight
out from it (a ray along its normal, within 6 cm) and keeps that cover's
local patch; per sample the signed distance to the patch's nearest deformed
face is measured. Testing against its own cover, not the whole garment,
keeps folds and openings elsewhere from hiding or inventing a failure.

**Carts.** Runners never ride carts in play (only the Night Watch does, in
its uniform), so no skin is ever seated in one; the cart clips are still in
the checks above (every clip is), and both skins pass them.

**Exception: Dr. Doom's hip crease.** In front of each hip joint, a thigh
raised well forward folds both layers at once; linear blend skinning
collapses the inside of that fold, and the trousers come through the front
skirt of the jacket there, typically 3–4 cm (worst 4.05 cm), in 34 of the 53
clips (strides at speed, jumps, landings, sitting). The check region is
small (82 trouser vertices in front of the hip joints, |x| 5–26 cm, z
0.48–0.585 m). Both layers are the same suit fabric and colour, the fold is
under the open cutaway front, and in the reels and close-ups it reads as a
fold of the suit rather than a fault (`fit_closeups_dorm.jpg`,
`actions_campus.jpg`, `reel_dr_doom.mp4`). It is reported as its own row
with its own bound so it cannot grow unnoticed. Removing it needs a hip
corrective shape or a helper bone (rig and clip territory: FIT stream or a
later pass).

**Arms near the heads.** Some existing clips bring the hands to the head on
purpose (shrug, shush, wave, dance, victory lap, stumble). Each skin is
measured against the stock head with the curly crop and the same arm radii
(Record Breaker's bare arms, Dr. Doom's jacket sleeves; the baseline is
measured with the same arm radii). Every clip
is within the baseline plus 1 cm, except the shush, which lays the mitten on
the lips by design: the skins' fuller noses and lips meet it 1.1 cm deeper
than the stock face does (Dr. Doom 4.1 vs 3.0 cm, Record Breaker 1.3 vs
0.2 cm); that clip has a 1.5 cm margin. Dr. Doom's jowls were moved forward and up during the
pass so the dance and shrug arms clear them.

Memory rows: [`data/memory.json`](../media/pass9/skins/data/memory.json),
`src/dev/skins_memory.tscn` (new): Godot's own allocation counters on the
desktop Mobile renderer (llvmpipe), each measured in a fresh process, the
"before" asset imported with the same settings. They compare before and
after on one machine; they are not a phone's resident memory.

Both skins are under the heaviest existing look with less than half its
draw calls, so the triangle and draw-call worst case for all eight players
is unchanged. The asset grows because the one shared GLB carries every part
(loaded once and shared; only worn parts draw), and the imported scene grows
faster than the GLB because the two heads' 18 face shapes are stored as
full blend-shape arrays and the importer adds LODs.

**Finding: every view pays for every part.** Each `CharacterView`
instances the whole asset and hides the parts its look does not wear, and
the renderer still allocates per-instance skinning buffers for the hidden
parts: the eight-player cost is about 21 bytes per asset vertex per view,
before and after (3.7 MB of GPU buffers per view before, 4.5 MB after),
whatever the looks are. So every part added to the asset, worn or not,
costs memory in every player's view: about half of the +12.7 MB above
(+6.7 MB) is that per-view cost; the other half is the asset itself. Instancing only the worn parts (or freeing hidden
ones and re-instancing on a cosmetic change) in `CharacterView` would
recover most of the per-view cost for every look. That touches the view's
model build and animation setup (FIT stream / integrator), so it is not
changed here (open item 4).

## Open items and owner dependencies

1. **Season stream (`p9-pass100`):** the catalogue items and tier 50/100
   rewards are on that branch. Until both are merged, `test_catalogue`
   reports the two `outfit:*` IDs as orphans on this branch alone, and that
   branch's Cosmetics-art check fails without this one.
2. **Hip crease (FIT / animation):** Dr. Doom's trousers fold through the
   jacket's front skirt in strides (≤ 4.05 cm, same fabric, under the open
   front). A hip corrective shape or helper bone would remove it.
3. **Movement stream:** the face's "focused" state reads the render state's
   `"sprinting"` flag, which the integrator renames to `"fast"`; the test
   sets both, so it passes before and after the rename.
4. **Device memory (integrator / FIT):** no phone was used. Triangles and
   draw calls are within the earlier heaviest look; the asset and the
   eight-player allocations grew by 12.7 MB on the desktop counters
   (Budgets), half of it because every view allocates skinning buffers
   for every part, worn or not. Recommended: build each view from its worn
   parts only; then measure on the oldest supported phone.
5. **Lobby flourish:** a lapel adjustment or cuff check for Dr. Doom would
   need new clips (animation stream); not added.
6. **Night Watch variant:** none (by design): a skin owner on the Watch wears
   the uniform with their own modular head.
