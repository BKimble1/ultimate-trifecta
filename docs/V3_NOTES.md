# V3 implementation notes

V3 adds modern character animation, a premium lobby, reliable parties and
App Store preparation. Gameplay rules are unchanged from V2: 6 runners
against 2 Night Watch, three of the six waters are tonight's targets, a 4:00
round, four runners home to win, stamps kept when caught, and a 6 s capture.
Animation never feeds back into the simulation.

What was verified, and how, is in [TEST_REPORT.md](../TEST_REPORT.md). The
release state and the owner's remaining steps are in
[TESTFLIGHT_RELEASE.md](../TESTFLIGHT_RELEASE.md). Licences are in
[ASSET_LICENSES.md](../ASSET_LICENSES.md).

## Characters (art)

The benchmark is the app icon: a soft, rounded, matte look with large clear
eyes. The character is still generated by the Blender `bpy` pipeline
(`tools/character/`) into one shared rig and asset contract.

- **Head and face.** The head is icon-proportioned. Eyes have a dark-brown
  iris, a near-black pupil and one large catch-light. Brows are tapered.
  Face shape keys: `blink`, `squint`, `smile`, `open`, `brow_up`,
  `brow_angry`, `face_bright`, `face_sleepy` and `brow_flat`. Skin is matte
  (roughness 0.78, low specular), which removed a chin hot spot.
- **Hair.** Hair is built as shells from explicit hairline curves
  (`hair_shell`), replacing per-face keep masks that gave a stepped hairline
  in play. Styles are short, bob, curly and buns, plus freckles.
- **Speckling, root cause.** Characters received the sun's shadow map. At
  phone resolution the self-shadow acne on rounded faces and collars read as
  stipple. Character materials now use `shadows_disabled` (they still cast
  shadows), and the collar's sawtooth stripe came from per-face stripe UVs.
  Stripes now follow world height on a larger shoulder cap. Nothing is
  hidden with blur.
- **Wet look.** An instance uniform `wet` darkens albedo and lowers roughness
  after a splash, then dries.

## Animation

All files are in `game/src/view/character_view.gd` and
`tools/character/anims.py`. The library has 40 clips. Emote loops are
seamless.

- **Gait.** Walk, run and sprint are timed from the asset's own stride:
  0.70, 2.20 and 2.75 m per cycle, with duty factors 0.58, 0.20 and 0.165.
  Playback rate is speed divided by metres per cycle, so feet don't slide.
  Blends between gaits share one phase (time-seek nodes, synced
  blend-space), so a speed change never restarts the cycle. Footsteps are
  fired from that phase at each foot's contact. A test checks cadence
  against the asset's measured reach (walk 0.427, run 0.448, sprint 0.441).
- **Turns.** Turns lean into the curve, reversals plant, and the visual yaw
  is smoothed at 24/s and never turns the long way round.
- **Air and impacts.** The jump arc is chosen by vertical velocity. Dives,
  soft and heavy landings, tag anticipation, lunge and miss all have clips.
  A cart pose holds the wheel; the cart lineup confirms hands on the rim.
- **Lobby.** Idles have per-character desync. There are arrivals, a ready
  response, celebrations and a leave animation. The selected emote is the
  ready move.
- **Recovery** after resurfacing is an upper-body layer (filtered
  one-shot), so legs keep running while the runner shakes off water.
- **Distant characters** advance manually at a throttled rate, but the
  throttle accumulates delta, so they keep real time. Render events such as
  landings and footsteps are never duplicated by a floor-contact flicker.

## Splash signature sequence

The splash runs on a 1.5 s timeline: contact, dip, tread, breath, then a
duck under. It is driven by the runner's **authoritative time in the
water** (`state_t` from the simulation). Late joiners and clients see the
same beat; they resync when more than 0.15 s off. The impact class (walk-in,
jump or dive) is computed in the sim before the velocity reset. It travels in
a new event byte and in snapshots, and the size of the crown or sheet
follows it.

- Effects are pooled (CPUParticles3D, at most 10 per kind) with soft radial
  textures. The water shader keeps 6 concurrent ripples (`vec4[6]`: x, z,
  age, strength).
- With Reduced Motion: smaller spray, fewer bubbles and drops.
- The sound comes from the event only, so it plays exactly once. The visual
  beats never play sound.

## Menus, lobby and typography

- **Type.** Fredoka is a variable font, and Godot rendered it at its
  default Light weight whatever weight was requested. We measured ink
  coverage: 1373, 1375 and 1375 for weights 300, 500 and 700. Static
  Medium, SemiBold and Bold instances are now generated with fontTools
  (`tools/fonts/make_static_fonts.py`) and measure 2526, 3284 and 3758.
- **Buttons.** Buttons have a raised face with a darker lip and sink when
  pressed. Layout never moves. A short spring plays on release, but the
  action fires on press. Transitions run 150–250 ms, and Reduced Motion
  keeps fades but drops slides and bounces.
- **Content-sized buttons fix.** These buttons no longer collapse to empty
  pills: ellipsis trimming had made their minimum width ignore the text.
- **Wordmark.** An original Ultimate Trifecta wordmark on the home screen.
- **Dorm.** A cozy late-night common room. A dorm shader adds wood grain
  and plank seams, fabric weave and sheen, wallpaper, glossy props and
  contact AO. A reading lamp and moonlight light the room. Departures and
  arrivals animate.
- **Lobby framing.** The camera frames 1, 2, 4 or 8 players. It eases over
  0.25 s, or cuts with Reduced Motion.
  - The camera stays in front of the group and turns toward an aim point
    left of the party panel. When it sat off to the side, back-row faces
    fell behind front-row heads.
  - It rises with the depth of the group.
  - The room is built larger than any framing, so the iPad 4:3 view never
    sees past the walls.
  - `test_lobby` renders at 2532×1170, 1334×750 and 2048×1536, with tall
    hats. It checks that both eyes, the mouth and the forehead of every
    face are clear of nearer heads and hats, and that both shoulders are
    in frame.
- **Play with Friends.** One primary action, Create Party. To join, you
  enter a 6-character code from an alphabet without look-alikes; parsing is
  strict, and only case, spaces and dashes are forgiven. A busy card has a
  Cancel button, and Back cancels too. Errors are specific: no such party,
  ended, full, in a round, different version, not allowed, check the code,
  no connection, update needed.
- **Lobby.**
  - The header shows the code with **Copy**, **Share** (the native iOS
    share sheet, `UTShare`) and **Invite** (Game Center friends).
  - Each slot has a portrait rendered off-screen from the player's look.
    The host has a badge.
  - The player sheet offers Hide emotes, Report…, Block and Remove (host
    only).
  - Bot fill is stated once.
  - Bots are named "Pillow · BOT" (no "Bot Pillow · BOT"), and their world
    tags only show up close.
- **Create Your Runner** replaces the wardrobe.
  - Categories: Outfit, Colours, Face, Hair, Hat, Shoes, Move. Each tile
    shows Wearing, Owned or a price.
  - You can drag to turn (right stick on a controller) and switch between
    Idle and Run previews. Apply is atomic.
  - A note warns when the chosen hat hides the hair.
  - On first launch it opens without Back, followed by the name sheet.
- **Appearance schema 2.** Stable explicit item IDs and a versioned wire
  format (magic `0xA7`, field IDs). Legacy V1/V2 looks migrate to the same
  appearance, and paid items stay owned.
- **HUD.**
  - Your stamp is a compact pill under the home counter (water icon, name
    and progress) that pops in, then fades. Reduced Motion: no pop.
  - The event feed sits on soft pills, at most three lines. Other runners'
    splashes into the same water share one line.

## Native share sheet (UTShare)

The share sheet is a small GDExtension in plain C and Objective-C
(`native/ut_share`). It is built against `gdextension_interface.h`, which is
dumped from the pinned 4.7.2 engine at build time. The abstract class
`UTShare` has two static methods, `share(text, url)` and `available()`. On
iOS it presents `UIActivityViewController` from the top view controller, as
a popover anchored mid-screen on iPad. Desktop and test builds use a stub
that reports no sheet, so Share copies the code instead and says so. CI
builds the Linux library for the tests, and an xcframework (device arm64,
simulator arm64 and x86_64) for the iOS export.

## Profiles, names and moderation

The service lives in `service/`; see [service/README.md](../service/README.md).

- **Sign-in.** Game Center sign-in is verified on the server against
  Apple's certificate. A Game Center ID or a name alone never
  authenticates.
- **Tokens.** Session tokens are bound to profile, bundle, environment,
  audience and expiry. Admission tokens are RS256, single use, and bound to
  the joiner and the room.
- **Client.** The client is the `Cloud` autoload.
  - On first launch you create a runner and pick a name. The name stays on
    the device until the service checks it at the first party.
  - Settings › Profile has Change name, Edit runner, Blocked players
    (unblock), Sign out and **Delete Game Profile**.
  - Delete Game Profile signs in with Game Center again and deletes online
    first. It wipes the device only after the service confirms; a failure
    keeps everything and offers Try again. With no service it deletes on
    the device only.
- **Fix: double sign-in.** A successful sign-in used to end in the error
  state, because the state was derived from itself, so every API call
  signed in twice. Fixed and tested.
- **Default names.** Generated default names now always fit the
  3–16-character rule. V2 could generate 17-character names such as
  "Splashy Walrus 56", which the new rules would show as "Player". Saved
  names that don't fit are migrated: the number is dropped, or else a new
  name is generated.
- **Links.** Support and privacy links appear only when the owner sets real
  ones in the service config. There are no placeholders. A minimum client
  build can gate online play.

## Parties and protocol 4

- **No orphan matches.** Game Center party matches use the code's
  `playerGroup`. Host and joiner `playerAttributes` (`0xFFFF0000` and
  `0x0000FFFF`) only complete a match when they OR to all ones, so every
  match contains its host.
- **Host binding.** Clients bind to the service-named host. Host-only
  messages from anyone else are dropped, and ANNOUNCE can't replace the
  host.
- **Admission.** The host verifies the admission token: signature, audience,
  expiry, code, joiner and single use.
- **Reconnects.** A reconnect needs the slot's rejoin key, so nobody can
  steal a slot.
- **Input hardening.** All reads are bounds-checked. START and RESULTS are
  validated. Each peer has rate limits and flooders are removed. Names from
  the network are sanitised.
- **Loading.** The round waits for every player's load acknowledgement (at
  most 15 s) before one shared countdown.
- **Party lifecycle.** The host heartbeats the room every 15 s, and the room
  service records the state. Switching to another party asks first.
  Leaving releases the player's room membership.
- **Desktop transport fix.** The ENet transport (desktop and LAN
  development only) no longer sends to a peer that has left, or to one
  still waiting for its disconnect, which raised engine errors when
  several clients dropped at once.

## Controls

- **One press queue.** Touch taps, keys and controller buttons feed one
  bounded, ordered, expiring press queue. The old controller bit
  accumulator merged a rapid jump-then-dive into one press; now a repeated
  action waits for the next tick on every device.
  - The queue is cleared on screen changes, pause, controller disconnect
    and backgrounding.
  - Keys typed into a text field never press anything, and the pause menu's
    A is Resume, not a jump.
- **Arbitration.** Sticks follow one active controller: the last one with a
  button press or a deliberate push. A finger on the touch stick owns
  movement. Drift can't switch device while a finger is down, and
  stick-driven switches are rate limited, so prompts don't flicker.
- **Curves.** Radial inner and outer dead zones on both sticks. Movement
  has a mild expo that widens the walking range. Look has an expo plus a
  short boost when held at full tilt.
- **Prompts.**
  - Xbox and MFi pads show letters, and PlayStation pads show the four
    symbols.
  - Nintendo and unidentified pads get **positional** glyphs, because their
    printed letters don't match the positions the game receives.
  - While a controller or keyboard is used, a compact hint list replaces
    the touch buttons.
- **Menus.**
  - Every menu screen is navigable with the d-pad. A test walks the focus
    graph from the opening focus and reaches every button.
  - Dialogs, sheets and popovers trap focus. Back closes them, and focus
    returns to whatever opened them.
  - A controller code pad replaces the text field, which leaves controller
    focus so it can never trap you.
  - In the creator, LB/RB (or Q/E) switch categories.

## Testing infrastructure

- `tools/run_tests.sh` now fails a test when any script error is raised
  during it, using a `Logger` registered with `OS.add_logger`. Before, such
  errors were printed and ignored.
- `test_focus` resizes the window to phone size: the headless default is a
  1280×1280 square, where nothing scrolls.
- `tools/capture_lobby.sh`, `tools/record_lobby.sh` and
  `tools/label_movie.sh` produce the lobby evidence and the labelled clips.
- `--capture=account|creator|results`, `--sim-pad=…`, `--gc-sim=ready` and
  `--ready-after=S` are labelled development flags for captures.

## Camera and performance

- **Follow anchor.** The camera follows the same interpolated render
  position the character is drawn at. The view never smooths position
  again. The only extra smoothing is a 0.06 s vertical damp on the pivot
  for steps and jumps.
- **Canopy: the porthole is gone.** When the follow camera passed through a
  large tree, V2 pushed the nearby leaves out onto a 2.6 m sphere around
  the camera. The screen became the inside of a green bubble, with only the
  circle around the character cut open: the "porthole".
  - Foliage closer than 3.2 m to the camera is now cut away cleanly in the
    camera pass. The edge is clean, with no dither or stipple. Shadows are
    unchanged.
  - The circle around the followed character stays, for canopy further
    away.
  - **Measured** on the same seeded Night Watch round (`--seed=21`, Movie
    Maker at 30 fps, identical game state frame for frame). The share of
    the frame covered by dark canopy, sampling every 30th frame up to frame
    1320:
    - average: 26.9% before, 4.6% after;
    - frames more than 25% covered: 16 of 45 before, none after;
    - worst frame: 90.0% before, 2.9% after.
  - The measurement counts dark under-canopy pixels only, so a lit canopy
    face blocking the view isn't counted. One frame (930) reads 2.7% before
    and 8.9% after: before, a lit canopy hid the right third of the screen;
    after, the tree's trunk and branches show there instead.
  - The runner clip on seed 11 differs by under 3% of pixels per frame
    (wind sway).
  - Before and after stills: `docs/media/v3/gameplay/canopy_*`.
- **Budget.** The worst-scene budget is reported as draw calls and
  primitives from the in-game diagnostics. Those counts don't depend on the
  device. llvmpipe frame times are **not** performance data. See the test
  report.
